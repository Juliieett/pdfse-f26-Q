# Nergi — Prototype Plan

## 1. Problem we are testing

Farmers may struggle to access reliable, locally relevant information when making crop-related decisions. Interviews suggest uncertainty about crop suitability, plant requirements, and plant problems can lead to wasted money and time.

**Evidence:** Interview 04 (Avto), Interview 07 (Chognari-1), Interview 10 (Manana).

## 2. Riskiest assumption

Farmers will actively use a simple tool that provides crop-related guidance relevant to their land and situation, rather than relying only on their existing sources of advice.

This is risky because farmers currently use neighbors, agro shops, sellers, and online videos. We do not yet know whether they want another source of information or trust it enough to act on its recommendations.

## 3. Target user

A small-scale farmer in Georgia who is considering planting a new crop or needs information before purchasing planting materials.

## 4. One action to test

Give a farmer a crop-related question and ask them to use the prototype to find the information they need before deciding what to do next.

Example scenario:

“I am considering planting a new crop on my land. I want to understand what I should check before buying seedlings.”

Observe whether the farmer can identify the relevant information and explain what they would do next.

## 5. Cheapest useful prototype

**Low-fidelity clickable mockup or paper prototype.**

Create three simple screens:

1. **Describe your situation** — select a crop or describe the decision.
2. **Get relevant guidance** — show the information a farmer should verify, such as local suitability, growing requirements, and questions to ask a seller.
3. **Next steps** — show what to check before spending money, including when expert advice or soil testing may be appropriate.

Use clearly labelled sample content. Do not present unverified recommendations as scientifically validated agricultural advice.
## 6. Test procedure

1. Recruit a farmer who has experience growing crops or is considering a new crop.
2. Explain the scenario without pitching Nergi or teaching the participant how to use the prototype.
3. Ask the participant to complete the task independently.
4. Observe where they hesitate, what they misunderstand, and whether they trust the information.
5. Ask what they would do next and whether they would normally use another source instead.
6. Record the participant's actual actions and comments.

## 7. Success criteria

The test passes if the participant can independently:

- Find information relevant to the scenario.
- Identify at least one important thing to verify before making a crop-related decision.
- Explain a concrete next step they would take.

Record task completion, requests for help, and any reasons for distrust.

## 8. Failure condition

The test fails if the participant cannot find relevant guidance without substantial help, does not understand how it applies to their situation, or prefers their existing workaround because the prototype does not provide credible or useful information.

A failed test is useful evidence. Record the reason and revise the assumption instead of interpreting polite interest as validation.

## 9. What we will learn

The test should help us determine whether farmers can use a simple guidance tool to support a real decision, what information they need, and what makes a source trustworthy.

It will not prove that farmers will pay for Nergi, that soil analysis is the central problem, or that a full digital product is viable. Those assumptions require separate validation.
